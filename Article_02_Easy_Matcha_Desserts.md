# Easy Matcha Desserts You Can Make in 30 Minutes

Matcha isn't only for drinking. Its earthy, slightly sweet flavor makes it a wonderful ingredient for simple desserts — ones that don't require baking experience or hours in the kitchen.

Here are four easy matcha treats you can prepare in 30 minutes or less. Each one is simple enough for beginners, and beautiful enough to share.

---

## 1. Matcha Energy Bites (No-Bake)

A small, satisfying snack — perfect for an afternoon pause or a mindful moment between tasks.

### Ingredients

- 1 cup / 90 g rolled oats
- 1/2 cup / 130 g almond butter (or peanut butter)
- 1/4 cup / 85 g honey
- 2 tsp matcha powder (about 4g)
- 1/4 cup / 40 g dark chocolate chips (optional)
- 1 tbsp chia seeds (optional)

### Steps

1. Sift the matcha powder into a large mixing bowl. Add the oats, almond butter, honey, and any optional ingredients.
2. Mix everything together until well combined.
3. Roll the mixture into small balls, about 1 inch (2.5 cm) in diameter.
4. Place on a lined tray and refrigerate for at least 15 minutes.
5. Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to one week.

**Makes about 14–16 bites.**

## 2. Matcha Panna Cotta

A silky, elegant dessert with a gentle matcha flavor. No oven needed — just patience while it sets.

### Ingredients (serves 2)

- 1 cup / 240 ml heavy cream (or coconut cream for a vegan option)
- 1/4 cup / 60 ml milk
- 2 tsp matcha powder (about 4g)
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 1.5 tsp powdered gelatin (or 1 tsp agar-agar powder for vegan)
- 2 tbsp warm water (for blooming gelatin)

### Steps

1. Sprinkle gelatin over 2 tbsp of warm water in a small bowl. Let it bloom for 5 minutes.
2. Sift the matcha powder into a saucepan. Add the milk and whisk until smooth.
3. Add the cream and sugar. Heat gently over medium-low heat, stirring constantly. Do not let it boil.
4. Once warm, remove from heat and stir in the bloomed gelatin until fully dissolved.
5. Pour into two small cups or ramekins. Let cool to room temperature, then refrigerate for at least 2–3 hours (or overnight) until set.
6. Serve chilled, optionally topped with a light dusting of matcha powder or fresh berries.

**Prep time: 10 minutes. Setting time: 2–3 hours.**

## 3. Matcha Yogurt Bark

A frozen treat that's as fun to make as it is to eat. Light, refreshing, and naturally beautiful.

### Ingredients

- 1.5 cups / 350 g plain Greek yogurt
- 1.5 tsp matcha powder (about 3g)
- 2 tbsp honey or maple syrup
- Toppings: sliced strawberries, blueberries, granola, coconut flakes, crushed pistachios

### Steps

1. Sift the matcha powder into the yogurt. Add honey and mix until evenly green.
2. Line a small baking sheet or tray with parchment paper.
3. Spread the matcha yogurt evenly across the parchment, about 1/4 inch (0.5 cm) thick.
4. Arrange your chosen toppings on the surface. Press them gently into the yogurt.
5. Freeze for at least 2 hours until completely solid.
6. Break into irregular pieces and serve immediately.

**Store any remaining pieces in a freezer-safe bag for up to two weeks.**

## 4. Matcha White Chocolate Truffles

A small indulgence with a beautiful contrast — the richness of white chocolate meets the earthy depth of matcha.

### Ingredients (makes about 12 truffles)

- 5 oz / 150 g white chocolate, finely chopped
- 3 tbsp / 45 ml heavy cream
- 1 tsp matcha powder (about 2g), plus extra for dusting
- A pinch of sea salt (optional)

### Steps

1. Place the chopped white chocolate in a heatproof bowl.
2. Heat the cream in a small saucepan until it just begins to simmer. Remove from heat.
3. Pour the hot cream over the chocolate. Let it sit for 1 minute, then stir gently until smooth and glossy.
4. Sift in the matcha powder and stir until evenly combined. Add a pinch of salt if desired.
5. Cover and refrigerate for about 20 minutes, until firm enough to scoop.
6. Using a teaspoon or small scoop, roll the mixture into small balls. Dust each one with a light coating of matcha powder.
7. Keep refrigerated. Best enjoyed within 5 days.

## Tips for Matcha Desserts

- **Always sift your matcha.** Matcha powder clumps easily. A quick sift before mixing ensures a smooth texture and even color in every recipe.
- **Don't overheat matcha.** High temperatures can make matcha taste bitter and dull its vibrant green color. When heating, keep temperatures gentle — warm, not boiling.
- **Start with less, taste, then adjust.** If you're new to baking with matcha, begin with the amount listed and increase next time if you want a stronger flavor.
- **Quality matters.** A smooth, vibrant matcha — like Shizune Matcha — makes a noticeable difference in both flavor and color. You'll taste the care in every bite.
